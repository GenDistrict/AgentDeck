# AgentDeck 0.1.199

AgentDeck 0.1.199 lets an agent drive the Chrome you already use — signed in, with your extensions — instead of a blank throwaway browser. It also rebuilds the connector panel, and fixes two moments where the app left you with nothing to do.

## Highlights

### Use your own Chrome, not a stranger's

Browser automation used to open an empty browser: signed out of everything, none of your extensions, every site treating it as a first visit. Anything behind a login was out of reach.

- **Your Chrome, your sessions.** Connect the browser you actually use, so the agent works with the accounts you are already signed into.
- **Chrome asks you first.** The connection is a normal Chrome permission prompt — you approve it, and you can see and revoke it later.
- **Four steps, each one visible.** Install, add, approve in Chrome, and a step that retraces what happened if it fails. No silent failure with nothing to do next.

### Connectors are readable again

The connector panel had grown into one long list where a single failure was hard to find.

- **Collapsible sections** so you can see the shape of what is connected at a glance.
- **Errors appear inside the section they belong to**, next to the connector that failed, instead of at the top of the page.
- **A GPT app failure no longer floods the screen.** When a connector returned an error page, the raw HTML was printed into the panel; now you get a short message.

### Two dead ends removed

- **When your PC is off, the phone says so.** The approval-waiting overlay used to cover the screen even when the computer was unreachable, so the only thing you could do was wait for something that was never coming. It now tells you the PC is off and offers to connect to a different one.
- **Pasting an image behaves the same either way.** Ctrl+V and the [Paste image] button produced different results, and pasting twice could overwrite the first image. Both paths now behave identically and every pasted image is kept.

### For phone users

The Android app can now reach the service. Its browser engine reports a different origin than the iPhone's, and that origin was not on the allow-list — so every request from the Android app was blocked before it left the phone. The iPhone was unaffected.
