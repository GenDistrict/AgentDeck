# thread0 0.1.217

thread0 0.1.217 makes Remote access recoverable instead of leaving people trapped in a stale PC or expired collaborator workspace. It also completes the move to thread0 identity across Google sign-in and Remote, and tightens the release gate so automated evidence can no longer be mistaken for physical-device validation.

## Highlights

- **Remote always leaves a way out** — when a PC cannot be reached, people can retry, refresh, choose another PC, or sign in with another account. Changing account now completes a real sign-out and returns to sign-in instead of preserving a dead connection screen.
- **Revoked access is reconciled end to end** — when an owner ends a collaborator's access, stale invitation cards and local grants are removed, active relay access closes, and the collaborator sees an accurate access-ended state. A collaborator with no personal PC can return to another invitation or change account without being redirected to a nonexistent machine.
- **Collaborator invitations use one coherent flow** — email invitations no longer fall back into personal-device pairing or show duplicate safety codes. Accepted collaborators join through their verified email grant, and the owner is notified when the collaborator actually connects.
- **PC trust follows the signed-in account** — an already approved PC reconnects without another code, while an unrecognized device identity still requires explicit desktop approval. Switching accounts clears the previous account's PC, key, discovery, and transport state.
- **Current thread0 identity in sign-in and Remote** — Google authorization uses the thread0 Remote domain, the simplified Remote icon is used by the PWA and favicons, and recovery and collaborator language is localized across nine languages.
- **Broader local-model discovery** — Ollama-style model stores are recognized from their structure, including locations used by tools such as Msty and AnythingLLM.
- **Stronger release evidence** — agent-capable checks and user-only physical-device scenarios are now recorded separately. Source-pattern checks cannot lock a behavioral regression, and a release cannot report a user-only scenario as passed without user validation.
